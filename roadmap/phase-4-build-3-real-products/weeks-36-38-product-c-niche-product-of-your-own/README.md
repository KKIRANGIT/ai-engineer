# Weeks 36-38: Product C - Niche Product of Your Own

Back to [Phase 4](../README.md)

## Goal

Choose one problem domain you understand and build a product that reflects your own product judgment rather than a copied category template.

This is the most important product of the phase because it tests whether you can make a differentiated choice.

## Why This Product Category Matters

Products A and B gave you strong build patterns. Product C tests something deeper:

- can you choose well
- can you scope well
- can you tie your technical strengths to a real niche

This is often the product that matters most for:

- long-term SaaS exploration
- consulting positioning
- portfolio uniqueness

## What This Product Should Prove

By the end of this track, your product should prove that you can:

- identify a painful repeated workflow in a real niche
- define a believable product scope around it
- choose the right AI pattern instead of defaulting to complexity
- connect the product to a strong user-value story

## Product Selection Filter

Use all of these filters:

- painful
- repetitive
- expensive when done manually
- frequent enough to justify software
- narrow enough for one person to ship
- understandable enough to explain clearly

Good product ideas often come from:

- your own work
- family or friends with domain pain
- local businesses
- communities you already understand

## Good Product Directions

Examples:

- exam-specific AI tutor
- clinic note summarizer
- contract clause reviewer
- internal knowledge assistant
- recruiting workflow assistant

The best choice is not the trendiest one. It is the one where:

- the pain is clearest
- the user is clearest
- the workflow is clearest

## Success Criteria For This Product

You should consider the product successful if:

- the niche choice is specific and defensible
- the workflow is clear
- the product delivers one clear outcome better or faster than the current manual path
- the system design matches the actual problem instead of showing off every technique

## What To Optimize

This product should optimize for:

- problem clarity
- workflow-product fit
- believable value proposition
- scope control
- differentiation from generic AI apps

## What To Learn While Building

This track should sharpen your ability to:

- choose a niche intelligently
- build around real pain instead of feature novelty
- constrain scope to something finishable
- frame a product for hiring, consulting, or monetization

## Execution Plan

## Week 36: Problem discovery and product framing

Focus:

- choose the niche
- interview or informally discuss the pain point if possible
- map the current workflow

Deliverables:

- product brief
- target user profile
- current workflow note

Questions to answer:

- what exactly is painful today
- how often does it happen
- why do existing tools not solve it well enough

## Week 37: Product build

Focus:

- implement the minimum credible workflow
- choose the right AI architecture
- make the core value loop real

Deliverables:

- working prototype
- first example usage flows

Questions to answer:

- does this need retrieval, tools, structured outputs, or just a strong prompt
- what is the minimum scope that still proves value

## Week 38: Validation, iteration, and strategic review

Focus:

- get feedback
- evaluate fit
- decide whether this product deserves deeper investment

Deliverables:

- live demo if feasible
- cost estimate
- user feedback notes
- next-step roadmap

## Build Requirements

At minimum, the product should include:

- clear workflow
- real or realistic user input path
- one meaningful AI-powered outcome
- enough structure to support a case study

Recommended additions:

- simple pricing thought experiment
- feedback form or notes
- cost estimate per workflow

## User Validation Expectations

Even a small amount of real validation matters here.

Try to learn:

- would someone actually use this
- what part of the workflow they value most
- what part feels untrustworthy or incomplete
- whether the niche is worth continuing

## Deliverables

By the end of this track, you should have:

- one niche-specific AI product
- demo or runnable workflow
- product brief
- user feedback notes
- cost estimate
- next-step roadmap
- case study

## Common Mistakes To Avoid

- choosing a niche you do not actually understand
- copying an idea because it is popular on social media
- making the product too broad
- adding complexity that does not support the niche workflow

## Expert Notes That Matter Here

### Niche clarity beats generic ambition

A narrow product with a strong value story is often much more credible than a broad, vague assistant.

### Product selection is itself a skill

If you can consistently choose stronger problems, everything downstream becomes easier to monetize.

### This track should influence your future direction

Do not treat Product C as just another build. Treat it as a strategic test of where you may want to go next.

## Final Standard For This Track

The correct outcome is not "I built my own AI idea."

The correct outcome is:

"I chose and built a niche-specific AI product with a clear pain point, a believable workflow, and enough strategic clarity to inform what I should pursue next."
