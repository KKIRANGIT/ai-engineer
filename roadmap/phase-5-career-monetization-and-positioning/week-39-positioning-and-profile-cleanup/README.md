# Week 39: Positioning and Profile Cleanup

Back to [Phase 5](../README.md)

## Goal

Present a clear, believable, and specific story about what you build, who it helps, and why your work deserves attention.

## Why This Week Matters

If your positioning is vague, the market has to guess what you are good at.

Weak positioning sounds like:

- "I do AI"
- "I build full-stack apps"
- "I am passionate about technology"

Strong positioning sounds like:

- the type of problem you solve
- the type of user or buyer you serve
- the type of system you are strongest at building

This week matters because every later phase-5 activity depends on clear positioning:

- resumes
- LinkedIn
- portfolio
- outreach
- interviews
- consulting conversations

## Week 39 Outcomes

By the end of this week, you should be able to:

- describe your positioning in one or two clear sentences
- update your public profiles to reflect that positioning
- choose which projects best support your story
- remove weak or confusing signals from your public presence

## What To Learn and Decide

## 1. Positioning formula

At a practical level, your positioning should answer:

- what do you build
- for whom
- for what kind of outcome

You do not need an over-polished slogan. You need clarity.

## 2. Credibility boundaries

Do not claim more than your proof supports.

Use:

- specific project types
- specific workflows
- specific technical strengths

Avoid:

- broad expert labels with no evidence

## 3. Project selection

Not every project should be equally visible.

Choose:

- the most coherent projects
- the most differentiated projects
- the projects that best fit your intended path

## Execution Plan

Update:

- LinkedIn headline

- pinned repositories
- portfolio intro section

Then write:

- one short positioning statement
- one 30-second verbal intro

## Deliverables

- updated LinkedIn summary and headline
- updated GitHub profile
- prioritized portfolio project list
- one clear positioning statement

## Exit Criteria

- someone can understand your direction quickly
- your strongest public work supports the same story
- weak or confusing project signals have been reduced

## Common Mistakes To Avoid

- claiming too much too broadly
- listing many tools without a value story
- keeping weak projects equally visible with strong ones

## Expert Notes That Matter Here

### Positioning is compression

You are making it easier for the market to remember and categorize you.

### Specificity creates trust

It is easier to believe a focused claim backed by proof than a broad claim backed by aspiration.

## Final Standard For This Week

The correct outcome is not "I updated my profile."

The correct outcome is:

"My public presence now communicates a clear, believable technical story that matches my strongest proof."
