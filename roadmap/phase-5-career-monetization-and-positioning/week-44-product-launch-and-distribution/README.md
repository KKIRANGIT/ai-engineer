# Week 44: Product Launch and Distribution

Back to [Phase 5](../README.md)

## Goal

Take your strongest product and expose it to real market conditions through a focused launch and initial distribution effort.

## Why This Week Matters

A product with no users teaches you less than a product exposed to real reactions.

This week is not about vanity launch theater. It is about getting:

- real signups
- real activation attempts
- real retention clues
- real user questions

## Week 44 Outcomes

By the end of this week, you should have:

- chosen one product to push
- improved onboarding and landing clarity
- launched it in relevant channels
- collected early usage and feedback data

## What To Learn and Do

## 1. Launch readiness

Before launch, confirm:

- the product promise is clear
- onboarding works
- error states are acceptable
- the first value moment is reachable

## 2. Distribution fit

Launch where your target users actually pay attention.

Not every product needs the same launch channel.

## 3. Market-signal interpretation

Look for:

- signups
- activation
- confusion points
- willingness to continue

## Recommended Daily Breakdown

### Day 1: Choose the product

### Day 2: Improve landing and onboarding

### Day 3: Set up launch tracking

### Day 4: Launch in targeted channels

### Day 5: Respond to feedback and bugs

### Day 6: Review activation and retention signals

### Day 7: Write launch lessons

## Execution Plan

Prepare:

- product page cleanup
- onboarding cleanup
- launch channel list
- basic launch metrics

Then launch and observe.

## Deliverables

- live launch attempt
- channel list
- early metrics note
- feedback summary

## Exit Criteria

- the product was exposed to real external attention
- you collected signal stronger than your own opinion
- you can describe what improved or failed during launch

## Common Mistakes To Avoid

- launching without fixing basic onboarding
- choosing channels unrelated to your user
- optimizing for impressions instead of useful behavior

## Expert Notes That Matter Here

### Distribution is part of product reality

If users cannot find or understand the product, technical quality alone will not rescue it.

### Launch data should drive iteration

You are not only shipping. You are learning what the market reacts to.

## Final Standard For This Week

The correct outcome is not "I posted my product somewhere."

The correct outcome is:

"I exposed one real product to real users, captured meaningful early signals, and learned something actionable from the launch."
