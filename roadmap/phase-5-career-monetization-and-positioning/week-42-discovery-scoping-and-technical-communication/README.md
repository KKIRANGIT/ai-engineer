# Week 42: Discovery, Scoping, and Technical Communication

Back to [Phase 5](../README.md)

## Goal

Learn how to run clearer technical conversations with employers, founders, or clients by asking better questions, identifying risk earlier, and defining scope more responsibly.

## Why This Week Matters

Good builders still lose trust when they communicate poorly.

Common weak patterns:

- jumping into tools before clarifying the problem
- agreeing to vague scope
- failing to surface data or workflow risks
- overpromising on timelines

This week is about becoming the kind of engineer who improves clarity during the conversation itself.

## Week 42 Outcomes

By the end of this week, you should be able to:

- ask better discovery questions
- clarify goals and boundaries
- identify risky unknowns early
- produce a more credible statement of work or technical scope
- explain build-vs-buy and risk tradeoffs clearly

## What To Learn and Practice

## 1. Discovery mindset

You are not there only to impress. You are there to reduce ambiguity.

Ask about:

- current workflow
- current pain
- success definition
- existing data and systems
- urgency
- stakeholders

## 2. Scope boundaries

A good scoping conversation defines:

- what is included
- what is excluded
- what assumptions are being made
- what dependencies exist

## 3. Risk communication

Strong technical communication includes:

- uncertainty
- dependency risk
- data quality risk
- timeline risk
- integration complexity
