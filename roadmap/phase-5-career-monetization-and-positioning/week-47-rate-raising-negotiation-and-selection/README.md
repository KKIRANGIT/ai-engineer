# Week 47: Rate Raising, Negotiation, and Selection

Back to [Phase 5](../README.md)

## Goal

Learn to choose better opportunities, communicate value more clearly, and avoid low-quality engagements that consume time without building momentum.

## Why This Week Matters

Once you have some proof and process, the next skill is selectivity.

Not every opportunity deserves a yes.

This week is about:

- pricing confidence
- opportunity filters
- scope realism
- protecting your time and reputation

## What This Week Is Actually Training

Week 47 is training five deeper skills:

1. evaluating opportunities rigorously instead of emotionally
2. connecting rate discussions to scope and proof
3. spotting bad-fit engagements earlier
4. using boundary language without defensiveness
5. choosing work that compounds your positioning

The real outcome is not "I thought about charging more." The real outcome is "I have a clearer filter for fit, price, risk, and when to walk away."

## Scope Boundary For This Week

This week focuses on:

- opportunity scoring
- pricing logic
- negotiation language
- walk-away criteria

This week does not require:

- aggressive sales tactics
- pretending every opportunity should be closed
- discussing price without scope clarity

The correct goal is better selection and stronger value communication.

## Week 47 Outcomes

By the end of this week, you should be able to:

- evaluate opportunities more critically
- explain what makes a project a good or bad fit
- discuss scope and price with more confidence
- identify when to walk away

## What To Learn and Practice

## 1. Opportunity filters

Evaluate by:

- budget
- problem clarity
- data access
- decision speed
- communication quality
- timeline realism

## 2. Rate and value communication

Rates should connect to:

- proof
- complexity
- urgency
- ownership

## 3. Selective yeses

Saying yes to bad-fit work creates:

- stress
- poor outcomes
- weak references
- blocked time for better opportunities

## Best Source Strategy For This Week

Use sources in this order:

1. the local Week 47 workspace
2. opportunities you have actually seen
3. your best evidence and delivery strengths from earlier phases

Avoid discussing rate in isolation from scope, risk, and fit.

## Common Mistakes To Avoid

- accepting vague scopes because you want the opportunity
- lowering standards just to stay busy
- discussing rate without discussing scope and risk
- treating every "maybe" as a project worth chasing

## Expert Notes That Matter Here

### Selectivity is part of professionalism

Good builders often improve fastest when they stop saying yes indiscriminately.

### Pricing is narrative plus proof

Rates become easier to defend when your value story is specific and evidenced.

### Boundaries protect quality

Saying no to bad-fit work often preserves the energy needed for better compounding opportunities.
